6. Assumptions and limits of this study

  1. Weather and prices of one historical year (2023); data: Synthetic demo data (not real market data).
  2. Perfect foresight of day-ahead prices for the whole year. Real trading earns less, so revenues are an upper bound for the day-ahead market alone.
  3. Only day-ahead arbitrage and PV shifting are valued. Balancing services (FCR, aFRR), intraday trading and capacity payments are not included.
  4. Price taker: the BESS does not change market prices. This makes revenue grow almost linearly with size, which overstates the value of very large systems.
  5. AC-coupled BESS. PV energy lost by DC/AC inverter clipping is not recoverable (a DC-coupled design could recover part of it).
  6. In hours with negative prices the PV plant is curtailed (no payment for feed-in).
  7. Round-trip efficiency 88% (AC to AC), usable state of charge 5% to 95%, max 1.5 cycles per day.
  8. Capacity fade 2.0% per year, revenue scales with it. No augmentation.
  9. CAPEX 180 €/kWh (nameplate) + 70 €/kW, OPEX 1.5% of CAPEX per year, 15 years, discount rate 7.0%. No taxes, financing or price escalation.
  10. Grid charging fee 0 €/MWh.
  11. Product catalogue is generic and illustrative; values are not from a specific vendor.

7. Method

For every option the hourly operation of PV plant and BESS is optimised over one year with a linear program (HiGHS solver): maximise market revenue subject to the PV energy balance, the battery energy balance with charge and discharge losses, power and energy limits, the grid connection limit and a daily cycle limit. The same PV plant without storage is the reference case; all values above are the difference to it. Equipment counts come from the product catalogue, and the cash flows over the project lifetime give NPV, IRR, payback and LCOS.
